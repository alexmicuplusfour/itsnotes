Belém

took tram 15E west along the river. about 25 minutes.

Torre de Belém: smaller than I expected from photos. but the context of it — sitting at the edge of the Tagus, at the exact point where ships used to leave for places that weren't on any map yet — makes it feel much larger than its measurements. history does that sometimes.

Mosteiro dos Jerónimos: vast. gothic-Manueline, which is its own strange hybrid style — Gothic structure with maritime and naturalistic ornament, columns carved to look like twisted ropes and coral. spent an hour inside. the cloisters especially — two stories of carved stone around a courtyard, completely out of proportion with anything else nearby.

Pastéis de Belém: obviously. 25 minute queue. got two. ate them at the wooden counter inside, dusted with cinnamon and icing sugar, still warm. justified.

the whole area carries a weight — the sense of being at a place where something enormous was decided, or departed from. I kept thinking about the ships and the people on them and all the things they didn't know yet.
